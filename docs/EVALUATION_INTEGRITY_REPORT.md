## 5. Evaluation Split & Split Hierarchy Findings

### Legacy Flaw:
In legacy versions of `core.py` and `main.py`, models were fitted on `x_train` (or CV folds of train) and evaluated directly on `x_test`. There was no intermediate validation split.
- **Model Selection Contamination:** `best_candidate` in `core.py` (line 964) and `best_result` in `main.py` (line 623) selected the top model using `max(results, key=...)` based on **metrics computed on the final test set `x_test`**.
- **ThresholdOptimizer Contamination:** `ThresholdOptimizer` was fitted and selected against the same test evaluation regime.

### Remediated Split Hierarchy:
The upgraded pipeline implements an isolated 3-way partition:
$$\text{Train (60\%)} \longrightarrow \text{Validation / Dev (20\%)} \longrightarrow \text{Locked Holdout Test (20\%)}$$
- Estimators are fitted strictly on `Train`.
- Threshold tuning and model selection are executed strictly on `Validation`.
- Final reporting evaluates candidate models strictly once on `Locked Holdout Test`.

## 7. Composite Score & "Welfare Loss" Audit

### Composite Score Audit:
- **Legacy Formula:** `Composite Score = Balanced Accuracy - 0.25 * Fairness Gap`
- **Finding:** The $0.25$ multiplier is an ad-hoc linear scalarization without empirical economic grounding or stakeholder utility elicitation. Presenting it as an objective measure of model superiority is methodologically unjustified.
- **Remediation:** Refactored into `user_defined_utility_score(balanced_accuracy, fairness_gap, alpha=0.25)` and documented as a user-configurable preference scalarization.

### "Welfare Loss" Audit:
- **Legacy Formula:** `Welfare Loss Proxy = max(0, Accuracy_baseline - Accuracy_mitigated)`
- **Finding:** Equating predictive accuracy loss with economic welfare loss is invalid in welfare economics. Fairness interventions alter allocation distributions, worker earnings, and customer delivery latency—none of which are captured by accuracy delta alone. Claiming "zero welfare loss" because accuracy increased is commercially and economically indefensible.
- **Remediation:** Renamed to `Accuracy Tradeoff Proxy` across codebase and documentation.

## 8. Causal Claim Audit & Limitations

1. **Post-Treatment Mediator Conditioning:** In the Adult income dataset, the causal estimator conditioned on `occupation`, `workclass`, `hours-per-week`, and `capital-gain`. In labor economics, these variables are post-treatment mediators through which education affects earnings. Conditioning on mediators blocks the total causal effect and risks collider bias.
2. **Synthetic Treatments:** For Delhivery and Amazon, the "treatment" variable is synthetic noise mapped to delivery route features. Estimating an Average Treatment Effect (ATE) on a synthetic variable has no physical or real-world intervention meaning.
3. **Causal Documentation:** Added [`docs/CAUSAL_ASSUMPTIONS.md`](file:///c:/Users/varad/Documents/GitHub/algorithmic-fairness-analysis/docs/CAUSAL_ASSUMPTIONS.md) detailing DAG identification assumptions (Consistency, Exchangeability, Positivity, SUTVA).

## 9. Negative Control Results

Executed negative controls using `src/sensitive_audit.py`:
- **`PERMUTED_SENSITIVE_ATTRIBUTE_CONTROL`:** Random permutation breaks all demographic associations. In synthetic logistics setups, permuted fairness gaps drop substantially, confirming that non-zero disparities in raw models are partially stochastic or feature-driven.
- **`RANDOM_GROUP_CONTROL`:** Establishes baseline stochastic disparity floor due to finite test sample sizes.
- **`FEATURE_DERIVED_GROUP_CONTROL`:** Deriving group labels directly from route feature splits produced artificial fairness gaps exceeding $0.90$, demonstrating how synthetic proxy generation creates artificial disparity.
